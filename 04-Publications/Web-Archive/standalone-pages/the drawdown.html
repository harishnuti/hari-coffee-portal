Phase I — Served Hot

ACT ONEEverything bright
arrives first.

In a fresh cup, the volatile compounds hit before anything else — citrus, florals, the loud and the obvious. The first months of the year were like that: everything new, everything vivid, nothing yet in proportion.

Chapter One — The Awakening

It began on a family holiday, with a cup that wasn't supposed to matter.

Ubud, Bali · 3–4 January

Seniman Coffee Studio, Ubud. A family trip, a casual order, a natural-process Kintamani that arrived clean, citrus-bright, quietly floral — and something clicked. Not the coffee itself, exactly, but the noticing of it. Why was this natural so clean when naturals are supposed to be wild? That one question became a notebook, the notebook became a method, and the method became this archive.

Two more cups that weekend set the baselines: a wet-hulled Sulawesi Toraja — all earth, cocoa and syrup, the old Indonesia — and a house-blend piccolo to calibrate what milk does to everything. Three cups in, the vocabulary of the whole year was already forming: process shapes the cup, and the cup changes as it cools.

Chapter Two — First Precision

One Kenyan cup taught the method.

Kyūkei Coffee, Singapore · 10 February

Kenya Kiamugumo AA. SL28 and SL34 genetics, washed clean, poured over a flat-bottom bed through 120mm hybrid burrs at 1:16. The cup was blackcurrant, lemon, citrus zest, a mineral finish — and it was the first time the archive's full discipline appeared on one page: dose, yield, temperature, burr geometry, drawdown time, and the acid named precisely. Not "bright" — phosphoric. The Kenyan signature, confirmed across every thermal phase.

This is the entry the rest of the archive is measured against. Precision wasn't the goal; it was the reward.

Chapter Three — The Subcontinent Lesson

Mumbai taught what roast can silence.

Subko · Grey Soul, Mumbai · March

Two Indian anaerobics, two disappointments — and one of the year's most useful lessons. Subko's Project Pearl was roasted so light (Agtron 89) that the fermentation esters never woke up. Grey Soul's "Raspberry in Loop" was developed so far the terroir went quiet under chocolate. Between them, a principle: process promises nothing; the roast decides what survives. The archive stopped trusting labels that month and started trusting the cup.

Chapter Four — The Immersion

Then March in Singapore, when it became a life.

Eleven cups · Eight cafés · 18–29 March

Eleven cups in eleven days. The pace of that fortnight never repeated, and never needed to — it was the month the palate built its map. Wush Wush at Apartment showed what tea-like clarity really means at 1:17. Kenya Ndiaini stacked redcurrant and elderflower on the Kiamugumo baseline. Beloya strawberries, Tiong Hoe's chocolate comfort, ASK's lychee-brandy co-ferment.

And woven through it, the entries that matter beyond the cup: 18 March, Narrative Coffee Stand — Aanya's first specialty filter. A natural Pacamara, stewed apples and pu'er tea, chosen deliberately gentle. Some audits log extraction physics; this one logged a beginning.

The month closed with a threshold: 29 March, the first Gesha — Wilder Garcia's Peru, flash-chilled at Asylum, jasmine held in ice. The apex varietal had entered the archive. It would not leave.

Not "bright." Phosphoric. The difference between those two words is the difference between drinking coffee and reading it.
On the Kenyan entries — February 2026

ACT TWOSweetness emerges

As a cup cools past the volatiles, the sugars step forward and the structure shows. April and May were the archive's mid-cool: the milestones, the discoveries, and — one Saturday in May — the cup of the year.

Chapter Five — The Milestone Month

April: rare genetics and a revelation across the border.

April · Singapore & Johor Bahru

On the first of April, two landmark cups in one lunch hour: La Laja, a washed Sudan Rume at Maxi Coffee Bar — raspberry and nectarine, extremely clear, a technical milestone through 120mm flat burrs — and the first Panama Geisha, red honey, at Lume. Four days later, back at Asylum with Aanya: a watermelon co-ferment and a dual-temperature Gesha, the 90-then-70 pour engineering the aromatics apart.

But the month's revelation happened across the causeway. 18 April, Johor Bahru: Hacienda La Florida, a Typica Mejorado through an Origami conical. Lime while hot. Distinct red plum as it cooled. A textbook citric-to-malic thermal transition, isolated so cleanly it read like a demonstration. The three-phase method wasn't a habit anymore. It was the entire point.

Lime when hot. Plum as it cooled. One cup, two coffees — you just have to wait.
Hacienda La Florida · Johor Bahru · 18 April
Chapter Six — The Golden Run

May: eleven cups, and the one that beat them all.

May · Ten cafés across Singapore

May was the archive's richest vein. Nylon's quartet in two days — Banko Gotiti's stunning arc from lemon to melon to white tea. El Morito at Alchemist, the citric-malic overlap so integrated the verdict simply reads: "Confirmed: I love this bean." Ijen Laurina's textbook hibiscus-to-honey progression. La Papaya at 2,100 metres — the highest-grown Geisha of the year, terpene-forward, nothing like its Peruvian cousins.

Then, 16 May, FLUID. El Obraje — a washed Huila Gesha on a flatbed dripper. Mango, orange blossom, nectarine, honey. The notes in the audit abandon all pretense of detachment. It was, and remains, the cup of the year.

Ten days later the pendulum swung to the opposite pole: Zerah's "Ester Bomb" — a yeast-anaerobic Caturra Chiroso that detonated passionfruit on arrival, turned funky mid-cup, and finished on quiet mango sugars. Three completely different cups in one glass. The archive had found both of its poles in a single month: transcendent clarity, and controlled chaos.

Damn, the taste is so nice — the orange nectarine finishing with that honey is terrific. So far, my favourite.
El Obraje · FLUID · 16 May — the cup of the year

ACT THREEWhat lingers

The finish of a cup hides nothing — the sugars, the flaws, the aftertaste that stays. June brought the archive's maturity: rare firsts, honest misses, and a manifesto about what a pourover actually is.

Chapter Seven — The Frontier

June: six firsts in thirty days.

June · Twelve cups · Nine cafés

By June the archive stopped collecting cups and started collecting frontiers. First non-Arabica: a Malaysian Liberica at a vinyl-record bar, root beer and rambutan, logged as a genuine delight rather than a curiosity. First Chinese origin: a Yunnan Purple Leaf Caturra, double-washed, a pandan-threaded surprise. First mosto fermentation. First Yellow Sudan Rume — a triple-milestone entry in a single cup. First Myanmar. First Pink Bourbon waiting just over the horizon in July.

June also sharpened the archive's honesty. Two V60 conical entries — Kurasu's Rwanda and PPP's Suke Quto — showed the same over-extraction fingerprint, and the audits say so plainly: hardware execution miss; terroir likely unrealised. A great archive isn't a highlight reel.

And on 30 June, at an automated tap-dispenser bar, the year's philosophical stand. The cup was technically clean. The verdict was a manifesto: pourover exists because a human reads the bed and responds. Remove the human, and even a flawless cup reads inert. Not worth a repeat — on principle.

A pourover is a conversation between a person and a coffee bed. Automate the person out, and what's left is consistent — and mute.
2050 Coffee · The Automation Verdict · 30 June
Chapter Eight — The Freshest Pour

And the archive is still warm.

Zerah Roasters · 3 July

The newest entry is three days old as this page is published: Finca Villarazo Pink Bourbon, washed, through 83mm flat burrs and an Orea V4. Pomegranate and mandarin arriving together, sugars surfacing exactly where the mid-cool should deliver them. And a human highlight: Aqil — the warmest, most generous barista of all forty-eight visits, explaining bloom ratios unprompted. The archive's next chapters are already being poured.

The Instrument

Palate DNA

Forty-eight cups reveal the taster as much as the coffee.

Signature acids
Malic first, always — the juicy, stone-fruit brightness that blooms mid-cool. Phosphoric respected in Kenyans; citric welcomed only in proportion; acetic tolerated as theatre, never as habit.
Signature aromatics
Linalool florals — jasmine, honeysuckle, white blossom — with stone fruit and white grape. When a cup shows these, the verdict warms noticeably.
Varietal apex
Gesha, seven entries and counting — with the rare-genetics court around it: Sudan Rume (both colours), Wush Wush, Pink Bourbon, Typica Mejorado, Purple Leaf Caturra.
Process loyalty
Washed clarity wins. Naturals earn their place; fermentation-heavy processes are logged with curiosity and rated with honesty — the Ester Bomb thrilled, but clarity keeps the crown.
Hardware conviction
Flat burrs, flat-bottom beds. Two V60 conical over-extractions in the archive versus a run of flat-bottom successes. The geometry argument is now data, not preference.
The comfort band
1:15 to 1:17 — where nearly every high-rated cup lives. Tighter for body, leaner for aromatic separation, and a firm belief that the barista's hand matters more than any number.
The ritual
Three reads per cup, no exceptions. Hot for the volatiles, mid-cool for the sugars, cool for the truth. A cup that doesn't change is a cup that's hiding something — or has nothing to say.
