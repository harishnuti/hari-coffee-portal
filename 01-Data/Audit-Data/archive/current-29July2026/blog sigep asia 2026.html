at the fair

My first coffee festival — and the two-hundred-year-old varietal that keeps finding me wherever I go.

16 JUL 2026SIGEP ASIA · SANDS EXPO, SINGAPOREENTRY LOGGED ⭐

I know how to walk a festival hall. Whiskey taught me that — the pacing, the shortlisting, the discipline of not spending your palate in the first hour. But in six months and sixty-five logged cups across Singapore, Bali, Mumbai and Johor Bahru, I had never once walked a hall built around the drink I actually keep a database for. SIGEP Asia was the first. Work took Day 1 from me; Day 2, I claimed three hours of an afternoon and went in with the archive open.

And the archive matters here, because this festival did not happen in a vacuum. It landed in the middle of the hottest fortnight my palate has ever had.

The stage

Watching the championship explains the archive back to me

The first thing that stopped me wasn't a booth — it was the Singapore National Brewers Cup, live. Competitors presenting filter coffee to a row of judges, and here is what nobody tells you until you stand there: the brew is only half the score. The narrative is judged. The theme. The way a barista frames a producer's story and defends a recipe. It's extraction as argument.

And the bean the arguments kept reaching for? Panama Geisha. Competitor after competitor. Watching that, something clicked into place: I've been mock-judged alongside Homeground's team, I've had national champions brew for me — Shen Choy's El Obraje, TaoTao's Torcaza — and every one of those pricey purple-tinted rows in my spreadsheet was never indulgence. It was the same bet the professionals make when a title is on the line.

I'll miss tomorrow's 4pm final for a meeting I can't move. It stings less than it should — because two hours later, a World Brewers Cup champion's roastery poured me their competition coffee anyway.

The purchase

MAME: Japanese name, Zurich soul, my name on the card

At the Roaster Village, a black-clad booth with a two-letter wordmark. MAME — I assumed a Japanese chain; the team smiled and said Switzerland. Both true, it turns out: founded in Zurich in 2016 by Emi Fukahori and Mathieu Theis, two baristas who met through championships. Emi is the 2018 World Brewers Cup Champion. Mame means "beans" in Japanese. A roastery bred entirely from competition — at a festival built around one.

I ordered the only cup on that board my Palate DNA could ever have chosen: the sole fully-washed Geisha with zero fermentation intervention. They handed it back with the bean card personalised in silver ink.

Seven grams, S$16 — the menu's ¥1,990 finally decoded (the S$25 next to it was for 20g of beans, not the cup). Scale that microdose up and you're at roughly S$34 for a full serving: on a full-cup-equivalent basis, the second most expensive coffee in my archive, behind only Torcaza's S$60 crown from two days ago. Two pours, a shallow bed, a short high-clarity extraction window: the physics were tuned for aroma over body, and the cup delivered exactly that.

The temptation

Timemore

Grinders, kettles, scales, full brew kits — the championship's own official hardware, laid out like a dare. I handled everything and bought nothing. Deliberately. One more year of drinking filters in cafés, asking baristas questions, logging every cup — and the archive itself will tell me exactly which kit deserves my counter. This spreadsheet isn't a diary; it's the research phase of that purchase.

The verdict

Whiskey taught me to walk a hall. Coffee taught me why I keep a map.

Six months ago the archive was a habit. This week it became a lens: it told me which of ten coffees on a menu board was mine before I tasted a drop, it placed a seven-gram festival pour instantly into a price hierarchy stretching back to January, and it stood me in front of a championship stage already understanding the game being played.

Next year I'll walk back into this hall knowing more. Maybe that's the year the Timemore box comes home. And in December, the map points somewhere new entirely — the estates of Coorg, where the archive finally goes bean-to-cup.
