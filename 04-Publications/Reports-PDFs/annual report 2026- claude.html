Taster Profile

The Palate Fingerprint

Across 31 rigorous audits spanning four cities and 17 cafes, a distinct palate signature has crystallized. Washed processes dominate the archive, reflecting a deep appreciation for transparency and the unadulterated expression of terroir. Hari consistently gravitates toward flat-burr grinders—whether the Lagom P64, CORE 54mm, or EK43S 98mm flats—prioritizing exceptional clarity and the precise separation of flavor layers over heavy body or muddiness.

High-altitude origins exert a magnetic pull. The 2,100 MASL Ecuador La Papaya entry stands as the pinnacle of the year, its terpene-forward profile (thymol, rooibos, apricot) revealing the aromatic intensity only extreme elevation can deliver. Colombia emerges as the strongest origin in the collection, with Wush Wush, Sudan Rume, and Sidra entries showcasing vibrant acidity, clean florals, and remarkable structural integrity. Geisha varietals consistently receive the highest praise, their genetic purity shining brightest when processing interference is minimized.

Thermal-shift awareness is perhaps the most sophisticated dimension of this fingerprint. Multiple entries document deliberate temperature cascades—92→75→92°C on the CORE, dual-temp on the EK43, multi-pour on Hario Alpha—that unlock evolving flavor arcs impossible to perceive at static temperatures. This is not mere technique; it is a philosophy of extraction that treats coffee as a dynamic, temperature-responsive system.

The data reveals a taster who values intellectual rigor alongside sensory pleasure: technical milestones (Sudan Rume isolation with 78mm SSP flats), processing experiments (anaerobic naturals, honey processes), and origin comparisons (Peru vs Ecuador Geisha differentiation) sit alongside pure emotional responses (“Damn the taste so nice… so far my favourite”). The archive is both laboratory and love letter to specialty coffee.

Extraction Science

Thermal Shift Hall of Fame

Five confirmed cases where deliberate temperature modulation revealed layered flavor arcs impossible at static brew temperatures. Chemistry: Different compounds extract and volatilize at different temperatures — citric acids early, malic mid-cup, heavier esters and sugars as the cup cools.

Field Notes

City Field Reports

Singapore — The Epicentre
23 entries • 13 cafes • May 2026 peak

The undisputed capital of this audit. From Nylon’s automated xBloom precision to Alchemist’s 98mm EK43S mastery, Singapore’s scene delivered unmatched diversity and technical excellence. 13 unique cafes audited across Raffles Place, Ocean Financial, Hamilton, and neighborhood gems. The city’s roasters and baristas consistently demonstrated world-class execution of both classic and experimental profiles.

Bali — The Foundation
3 entries • Seniman Coffee Studio • Jan 2026

The origin story. Three foundational entries at Seniman established baseline understanding of Indonesian naturals (Bali Karana Kintamanis) and wet-hulled structural density (Sulawesi Toraja). House Blend provided the espresso/milk pairing benchmark. Clean citrus-floral clarity in naturals proved Indonesian origins can compete with washed East Africans when processed with intention.

Johor Bahru — The Day Trip
3 entries • Quick cross-border forays

Three efficient day-trip stops (Hacienda La Florida at Mid Valley, Kenya AA at KSL, plus one co-ferment) revealed accessible specialty coffee culture just across the causeway. The standout — Hacienda La Florida Typica Mejorado — delivered one of the year’s most dramatic thermal shifts, proving JB cafes can punch above their weight with the right lots.

Mumbai — The Experiment
2 entries • Subko & Grey Soul • Mar 2026

India’s emerging scene delivered bold experimentation. Subko’s Project Pearl Ratnagiri showed clean balance despite intense anaerobic processing. Grey Soul’s Raspberry in Loop demonstrated how heavy multi-stage fermentation can mute brightness — a valuable cautionary lesson in processing-driven identity versus terroir expression.
