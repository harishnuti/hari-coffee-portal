// 07 · Technical Enrichment
Mosto Process Chemistry
Mosto fermentation introduces cherry pulp juice as the fermentation medium in an anaerobic environment. The pulp's natural sugars and wild yeasts drive controlled fermentation — more precise than a full natural, more complex than washed. Output: restrained acetic acid, elevated fruit ester production (isoamyl acetate = raspberry), vinous character from ethyl acetate accumulation.
Thermal Calibration Logic
88°C entry temp (vs 91°C on Entry #31 Gesha) deliberately suppresses acetic acid volatilisation at first contact — allows isoamyl acetate fruit esters to dominate the hot phase. The drop to 70°C for the finish arrests extraction before chlorogenic acid bitterness develops, locking aromatic stability and keeping the winey character integrated rather than sharp.
Hardware Analysis
Ditting 98mm flat burrs produce a tight unimodal particle distribution — minimal fines fraction. This is the key variable that licensed a tight 1:14 ratio without astringency; coarser adjusted grind vs Entry #31 to compensate for mosto bean's higher soluble density. Hario Switch immersion phase built body and sweetness evenly before the valve-open percolation drain added definition to the finish.
Archive Context
Entry #37 is the first mosto fermentation entry in the archive. Prior fermentation entries: Entry #28 Nicaragua Buena Vista (Anaerobic Natural, espresso) and multiple co-ferment entries.
