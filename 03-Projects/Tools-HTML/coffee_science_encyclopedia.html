PART 1 — ACID CHEMISTRY ATLAS

Chemical signatures that define every cup in the archive

Citric Acid C₆H₈O₇

Sensory: Bright, sharp, lemon-lime, grapefruit. High volatility when hot.
Hot → Cool Shift: Dominates hot; fades as it volatilises, revealing malic underneath.
Origins/Processes: Kenya AA (washed), Ethiopia heirlooms, high-altitude Geishas. Prominent in light roasts.
Milk Interaction: Cuts through fats, creates bright contrast in flat whites (see Agora Nicaragua entry).
Optimal Temp: 92–96°C initial extraction; lower temps mute it.
Archive Evidence: Kenya Kiamugumo AA (Kyuukei) — "Excellent baseline for clarity." Hacienda La Florida — "Lime when hot, distinct plum as it cooled."

Malic Acid C₄H₆O₅

Sensory: Green apple, plum, apricot, peach. Softer, rounder mouthfeel than citric.
Hot → Cool Shift: Emerges as citric fades; stable through cooling. Creates "apple shift" in Ecuador/Panama entries.
Origins/Processes: Ecuador high-altitude Geisha, Brazil naturals (Arara), Colombia Sudan Rume.
Milk Interaction: Binds beautifully with milk fats; creates long, comforting finish (Nylon Serra dos Ciganos).
Optimal Temp: 75–85°C perceived; triple-cascade preserves it (Pocket by Flip Ecuador entry).
Archive Evidence: Brazil FAF Organic Arara (Nylon) — "Crisp green apple (malic) front, resolving into smooth milk chocolate." Ecuador La Papaya — "Malic apple shift + caramelised sucrose."

Thymol / Terpenes C₁₀H₁₄O

Sensory: Rooibos, lemon thyme, herbal, medicinal, floral-terpene coat. Heat-sensitive.
Hot → Cool Shift: Unlocked at high temp (92°C), persists mid-cup, fades cleanly. Requires triple-temp cascade.
Origins/Processes: Highest-altitude Ecuador (2100+ MASL Loja), certain Panama Geishas.
Milk Interaction: Lipid-binding; creates persistent mid-cup coat (rare in milk drinks).
Optimal Temp: Triple cascade 92→75→92°C (Hario Alpha flat-bottom) to preserve volatiles.
Archive Evidence: Ecuador La Papaya Geisha (Pocket by Flip) — "Mid: thymol terpene coat — lipid-binding persistent to midway. No linalool/jasmine — distinct from Peru Geisha entries."

Other Key Acids in Archive

Tartaric: Apricot, structured elegance (Panama Corpachi Geisha — "Apricot detected clearly. Structured and elegant"). Stable, contributes to body roundness in honey processes.
Phosphoric: Cola-like brightness (rare in archive; subtle in some Kenya washed). High clarity but can feel sharp if over-extracted.
Acetic/Lactic: Fermentation-driven (Anaerobic entries like Nicaragua Buena Vista — "Highly fermented, savory/not sweet"). Adds complexity but risks muting terroir if excessive (Grey Soul Raspberry in Loop).
Chlorogenic: Bitter, astringent precursor in green beans; degrades in roast. Contributes to "earthy structures" in wet-hulled Sulawesi Toraja.

PART 2 — VARIETAL GENETICS DATABASE

Genetic lineage and flavour chemistry of every audited lot

Geisha (Gesha)

Lineage: Ethiopian landrace (Gori Gesha forest) → Panama (Boquete) → global. Typica/Bourbon hybrid.
Chemistry: High linalool (floral/jasmine in Peru), thymol (herbal in Ecuador), delicate acidity. Altitude-sensitive — peaks 1800–2100 MASL.
Best Processes: Washed, Red Honey. Avoid heavy fermentation (mutes genetics).
Hari's Archive: Peru Gesha (Wilder Garcia, Asylum) — "Clean, structured, high-clarity cup. High transparency; minimal processing interference highlighting intrinsic genetic purity." Ecuador La Papaya (Pocket by Flip) — "Terpene-forward... distinct from Peru Geisha entries." Panama Corpachi (Lume) — "Structured and elegant — sweet without being cloying."

Sudan Rume

Lineage: Ethiopian heirloom (Rume Sudan) × Typica. Rare, low-yield, high-value.
Chemistry: Intense floral (nectarine, raspberry), high malic, delicate body. Extremely altitude-sensitive.
Best Processes: Washed (preserves delicacy). Flat-bottom geometry essential for clarity.
Hari's Archive: Colombia La Laja Sudan Rume (Maxi Coffee Bar) — "Successfully isolated the intended tasting notes. Technical milestone in auditing rare genetics with 78mm SSP flat burrs."

Wush Wush

Lineage: Ethiopian landrace (Wush Wush forest, Gedeb). Bourbon/Typica hybrid, tea-like genetics.
Chemistry: Extreme tea-like clarity, low body, high volatile florals. Delicate — easily over-extracted.
Best Processes: Washed only. Light roast, precise flat-bottom pour-over.
Hari's Archive: Colombia Wush Wush (Apartment Coffee) — "Incredible tea-like clarity. Mapped the delicate genetics of Wush Wush."
Arara (Brazil): Bourbon × Yellow Catuai. High malic, crisp apple, excellent structure in naturals. Archive: Brazil FAF Organic (Nylon) — "Crisp green apple (malic) front... excellent structural body."
Parainema (Nicaragua): Sarchimor hybrid (Timor × Villa Sarchi). Thick body, savory esters in anaerobic. Archive: Nicaragua Buena Vista (Nylon) — "Thick, syrupy body. Highly fermented, savory/not sweet."
Heirloom (Ethiopia): Landrace mix (Guji/Yirgacheffe). High citric + floral. Archive: Ethiopia Banko Gotiti (Nylon) — "Lemon front, heavy melon mid-cup, fading into clean white tea finish."
Typica Mejorado (Ecuador/Colombia border): Improved Typica. High clarity, citric→malic shift. Archive: Hacienda La Florida — "Excellent. Lime when hot, distinct plum as it cooled."

PART 6 — THERMAL PHASE SHIFT GUIDE

The chemistry of hot-to-cool flavour evolution

Why the Citric → Malic → Sweet Transition Happens

Citric acid is highly volatile and evaporates quickly as the cup cools (vapour pressure drops). Malic acid is more stable and becomes dominant. Sucrose begins caramelising at lower perceived temperatures (~60–70°C), adding perceived sweetness. This is why dual- and triple-temperature strategies work so well.

Flat-Bottom vs Conical Timing

Flat-bottom (Hario Alpha, xBloom) slows initial flow, allowing more even heat transfer and better preservation of heat-sensitive terpenes (Ecuador La Papaya entry used triple cascade 92→75→92°C). Conical (V60, Origami) flows faster; temperature shifts must be more aggressive to achieve the same separation.

Ratio Impact

Lean ratios (1:15–1:17) accelerate the shift by reducing total dissolved solids early, letting malic and caramel notes emerge sooner. Tight ratios (1:14) like Brazil Santuário Sul maximise body but compress the thermal window.

FIELD GUIDE — 2-Minute Interval Protocol

  • 0:00–2:00 (Hot): Citric dominant — note lemon/lime brightness. (Hacienda La Florida: "Lime when hot")
  • 2:00–5:00 (Mid): Malic emerges — apple/plum transition. (Ecuador La Papaya: "Malic apple shift")
  • 5:00–10:00 (Cool): Caramel/sweet finish + body. (Nylon Arara: "resolving into smooth milk chocolate")
Archive Masters of Thermal Shift: Hacienda La Florida (Typica Mejorado, Origami + EK Omnia), Ecuador La Papaya (Geisha, triple-cascade Hario Alpha), Ethiopia Banko Gotiti (Heirloom, xBloom 1:17 lean).

PART 7 — PROCESS CHEMISTRY GUIDE

What each process does to acids, sugars, and body

Washed

Removes mucilage via fermentation + washing. Preserves varietal clarity, minimises fermentation esters. High transparency. Archive: Peru Gesha, Ethiopia Banko Gotiti, Colombia Wush Wush — "High transparency; minimal processing interference."

Natural

Whole-fruit drying. Sugars migrate into bean. High body, malic dominance, fruit-forward. Risk of over-fermentation. Archive: Brazil Arara (Nylon) — "Solid body with crisp green apple... Arara natural processing yielded excellent structural body."

Red Honey

Partial mucilage removal + drying. Adds roundness without heavy fermentation noise. Archive: Panama Corpachi Geisha (Lume) — "Red Honey process added roundness without ferment noise. Body rounder than expected for a washed."

Anaerobic (Natural / Washed)

Sealed fermentation produces esters, alcohols, CO₂. Can create savory or layered complexity but risks muting terroir. Archive: Nicaragua Buena Vista Parainema — "Anaerobic fermentation metabolized sugars into complex savory esters." Grey Soul Raspberry in Loop — "Heavy fermentation can mute terroir."

Wet-Hulled (Giling Basah)

Indonesian tradition: hull while wet, rapid drying. Earthy, heavy body, cocoa notes. High structural density. Archive: Sulawesi Toraja (Seniman) — "Heavy body with rich cocoa notes. Demonstrated the structural density of the wet-hulled process."
